# 04 — Frequency & Sound Claims

Claims that specific audio frequencies affect the body, DNA, emotions, or consciousness. Sources are mostly Instagram accounts (5dwaves, getsoundrx, spooky2rife, imzephyy, etc.). Evaluated via [`../00-method`](../00-method/); raw summary in [`sources/frequencies_summary.md`](sources/frequencies_summary.md).

## Claims index

| Claim | Source | Status (initial — verify) |
|-------|--------|---------------------------|
| Solfeggio frequencies (528 Hz "repairs DNA", 396 Hz "removes fear", 963 Hz "connects with spirit", etc.) | 5dwaves, qhht, miaross | Unsupported; no controlled evidence that tones repair DNA. The 432 vs 440 Hz tuning debate is real musicology — the *health* claims are the unsupported add-on bolted onto it |
| Brainwave entrainment → lower cortisol, better sleep, more energy | mr.highlev... | **Mixed** — entrainment is a real, studied effect on brain rhythms, but the specific health outcomes are mostly overstated beyond what studies show. Good case for "trace to the real research and see where it stops" |
| 40 Hz gamma "activates microglia, clears brain plaques" (cites MIT) | getsoundrx | **Mixed / important** — real MIT (Tsai lab) research does exist on 40 Hz gamma stimulation and Alzheimer's pathology, but largely in mouse models and specific conditions; the social-media version overreaches. Read the actual papers before judging either way |
| "Organ frequencies" (kidney 319.88 Hz, heart 67 Hz, thyroid 741 Hz, etc.) | spooky2rife (Rife-device lineage) | Unsupported; Rife-frequency claims have no clinical evidence base |
| Hawkins "Map of Consciousness" (log scale 0–1000, calibrated by muscle testing) | imzephyy | Unsupported; the calibration method (applied kinesiology) doesn't reproduce under blinded testing |

## Why this folder is a good test of the method

Not everything here collapses the same way. Solfeggio "DNA repair" and Rife "organ frequencies" have no evidence behind them. But brainwave entrainment and 40 Hz gamma sit on top of **real** research that the posts then stretch past what it actually showed. The skill isn't "reject all of it" — it's going to the primary study and marking exactly where the real finding ends and the social-media claim begins.
